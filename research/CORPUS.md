- **Compiled:** 2026-09-24, from six fact-checked lane files in `research/raw/` (bio-status, books, frameworks-marketing, frameworks-business, genz-translation, landscape-legal).
- **Rule for sections 1-8:** only claims marked CONFIRMED or CORRECTED in the lanes appear here, in corrected form. Everything else sits in section 9.
- **Copyright rule:** all content is paraphrased. The few short quotes (under 15 words) are ones where the exact wording is the fact, and each carries its URL.
- **The local persona file** (`~/.claude/skills/dan-kennedy-advisor/`) is not a source. Its "1948-2023" lifespan is wrong on both ends (see 1.1).
- **Self-reported numbers** (speech fees, books sold, members) are the company's marketing copy. The site must attribute them ("his company says...").
- **This is research, not legal advice.** Section 8 needs a lawyer's review before launch.

## 1. Status & bio

### 1.1 Alive or deceased (state it exactly like this)

**No death has been reported as of 2026-09-24.** No obituary, official death notice or reputable press report of his death exists for this Dan Kennedy. His Library of Congress name record gives no death date.

- **Birth date:** 1954-12-22, from the Library of Congress name authority record n87930166 ("Kennedy, Dan S., 1954-"). The date comes from a publisher's CIP data sheet. The record was last revised 2025-08-05 and has no death date. Wikidata Q100974018 gives the same birth date and no death date. - https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/n87930166.json ; https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:EntityData/Q100974018.json
- **Latest dated evidence of his own activity:**
  - April 10, 2026: his newest book, *The Business Owner's Emergency Survival Guide For the A.I. Revolution*, came out (Micro Publishing Media). - https://www.amazon.com/dp/1953321305
  - April 27, 2026: he was the guest on episode 217 of The Renegade Lawyer Podcast (host Ben Glass), talking about that book. - https://therenegadelawyerpodcast.buzzsprout.com/2044251/episodes/19072966-ep-217-dan-kennedy-on-ai-the-fastest-way-for-lawyers-to-become-invisible
  - May 19, 2026: a press release said he would co-host the free "Seven Figure Secrets Challenge" (May 18-22, 2026) with "co-owner" Russell Brunson and appear live on Day 5. Nobody has confirmed the appearance happened. - https://kdhnews.com/online_features/press_releases/magnetic-marketing-launches-free-seven-figure-secrets-challenge/article_0bc8e582-af30-51b8-b5a8-ef5e1c0f62bb.html
  - The podcast was still publishing new episodes through Sep 18, 2026, but some are probably archive recordings. - **Where the death rumors come from:**
  - **The 2019 hospice scare.** In August 2019 he was in hospice. His own letter dated 8/21/19 blamed overlapping infections, surgeries, and diabetic and heart conditions, and said he expected to die within days. Early tributes followed, including Joe Vitale's "The Passing of Dan Kennedy". - http://web.archive.org/web/20201111224950/https://dankennedytribute.com/ ; https://www.mrfire.com/marketing/the-passing-of-dan-kennedy/
  - **He survived.** On 10/7/19 he said he had left hospice and started new treatment and rehab, and that the outcome was not guaranteed. - https://www.mrfire.com/marketing/dan-kennedy-update/
  - **A different man's obituary.** AI summaries and an SEO blog claim he died Jan 15, 2024 of cancer. No source supports this. That date belongs to other men named Dan Kennedy: a 75-year-old Frito-Lay retiree from Illinois (born around 1948), and a Daniel F. Kennedy (1962-2024). The persona file's "1948" probably comes from this mix-up. - https://www.pitchfordfuneralhome.com/obituaries/Dan-Kennedy?obId=30416090 ; https://calvertmemorial.com/obituaries/daniel-f-kennedy
- **Evidence against a 2023 death:**
  - On May 19, 2023, Lee Milteer announced the "Not Dead Yet Tour", his first in-person solo event since 2019, set for July 24-26, 2023 in Cleveland. - https://milteer.com/not-dead-yet-tour/
  - On Jan 22, 2025, Russell Brunson said he interviews Kennedy regularly. - https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/dan-kennedys-blueprint-for-one-to-many-selling-sales-ep-03/id1315130618?i=1000684995240
- **Site wording to use:** "No death reported as of September 2026." Do **not** write "alive and active today". Nothing verified proves he is alive on 2026-09-24.
- **Name-collision warnings:**
  - English Wikipedia's "Dan Kennedy (author)" is a humorist tied to The Moth. - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Kennedy_(author)

Kennedy** (a Canadian copywriter who joined Lord & Thomas in Chicago in 1904 under Albert Lasker) is the one who defined advertising as "salesmanship in print" and created "reason-why" copy. ### 1.2 Personal facts (confirmed)

- **Home.** He lives in Northfield, OH, per his current Simon & Schuster author page. - https://www.simonandschuster.com/authors/Dan-S-Kennedy/264550355
  - In 2011 he said he lived in Ohio and Virginia. AWAI's bio (around 2016) says he had homes near Cleveland and Washington DC. - https://ilovemarketing.com/episode-16the-one-with-dan-kennedy/ ; https://www.awai.com/bio/dan-kennedy/
- **Hometown.** He called himself "from Bath, Ohio" in a 2011 interview with Gary Halbert. His exact birthplace is **not verified** (see section 9). - https://ilovemarketing.com/episode-16the-one-with-dan-kennedy/
- **Education.** He called himself a high school graduate in a Peter Lowe SUCCESS-event speech. - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/06-dan-kennedy-selling-from-the-stage
- **Family.** His wife is Carla, named in Adam Witty's Aug 21, 2019 letter. AWAI's bio jokes that he lives with his "2nd and 3rd wife (one in the same)" and a "Million Dollar Dog". - http://web.archive.org/web/20201111224950/https://dankennedytribute.com/ ; https://www.awai.com/bio/dan-kennedy/
- **Cars.** AWAI says he drives four collector cars, including a Rolls-Royce once owned by Dean Martin. - https://www.awai.com/bio/dan-kennedy/
- **Harness racing.** He owned Standardbred harness-racing horses and drove them in races. AWAI (around 2016) says he owned 20+ horses and drove 150-200 races a year. Whether he still races after losing his sight is unknown. - https://www.awai.com/bio/dan-kennedy/
- **Health.** He has documented diabetes. A complication required eye surgery, and by Dec 2019 he called himself functionally blind. By June 2020 he had lost nearly all his vision and read with a large magnifying glass. - http://web.archive.org/web/20201111224950/https://dankennedytribute.com/ ; http://web.archive.org/web/20210817223641/https://magneticmarketing.com/2020/06/23/dan-kennedy-is-alive/ ; https://milteer.com/from-grief-to-gratitude-for-dan-kennedy/
- **No-tech brand.** His company presents him as a little grumpy, a no-B.S. straight shooter, and someone with no email, no cell phone and no social media, reachable only by fax. It says these traits are genuine. - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/the-importance-of-brand-building-vs-direct-response-advertising
  - Ryan Deiss recalled the fax-only rule feeling outdated "even in 1999". - https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/thank-you-dan-kennedy-ryan-deiss
- **Phoenix years (added by critic).** In his "Selling From the Stage" talk he says he lived in Phoenix, Arizona, and jokes about its July-August heat. The years are not stated (single source, his own words). - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/06-dan-kennedy-selling-from-the-stage
- **Source-independence note (added by critic).** The birth date's two sources (LOC and Wikidata) may not be independent; Wikidata may simply reuse the LOC data. Treat it as one strong primary source.
- **People come to him.** He rarely travels, and clients come to him, usually in Cleveland. In 2011 he said he flew only private and rarely left because of his racehorses. - https://ilovemarketing.com/episode-16the-one-with-dan-kennedy/ ; https://www.awai.com/bio/dan-kennedy/

## 3. Marketing & copy frameworks

Format for each: **name**, then what it is (my words), then the source, then the Gen Z / 2026 translation. "Company copy" means Magnetic Marketing or GKIC house content, not Kennedy's own prose.

**M1. The Marketing Triangle: Message, Market, Media**
- **What it is:** Marketing has three parts: the message, the market that receives it, and the media that carries it. None works without the other two. Choose media by what the target customer actually uses and responds to, not by the owner's own habits.
- **Source:** *No B.S. Direct Marketing* excerpt, Entrepreneur.com, 2018-07-17. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/the-3-ms-of-successful-direct-marketing-campaigns-new/313518
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Pick the platform your buyer lives on before you write a word. TikTok and YouTube for discovery, Instagram DMs for conversion, Discord for community. Don't pick the one you personally like scrolling.
- **Note:** Adding "math" as a fourth M appears only in a homepage customer testimonial. Don't present it as his official framework.

**M2. Market first, then message; cut dead-weight prospects**
- **What it is:** Choose the audience before writing the message; most people do it backwards. Find which prospects you can stop spending on. His example: a mailer sending about 200,000 pieces a month found about a dozen weak states. Another: a chiropractor who targeted Amex holders who read Prevention magazine got far better response.
- **Source:** Kennedy/Glazer podcast (2022); archived talk; the 3 Ms excerpt. - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/how-to-create-a-compelling-message-that-practically-forces-prospects-to-stop-what-they-re-doing-and-take-notice ; https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/11-advanced-magnetic-marketing
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Niche down hard. Check your analytics for which audiences and regions actually buy, and stop paying to reach the rest.

**M3. Message-to-market match and the A-pile**
- **What it is:** Mail lands in an A-pile (read now), a B-pile (maybe later) or the trash. Getting into the A-pile depends first on message-to-market match, specific relevance and goodwill. Delivery tricks (FedEx, lumpy packages, teaser copy) come second. Without that match you're a pest, and sending more often makes response worse.
- **Source:** Marketing Speak ep. 61 (2016-12-21); podcast premiere (2022). - https://www.marketingspeak.com/getting-into-the-a-pile-dan-kennedy/ ; https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/ep_01-welcome-to-dan-kennedy-s-magnetic-marketing-podcast
- **Gen Z / 2026:** The feed is the new mailbox. The A-pile is the first 3 seconds (TikTok says to state the content proposition in 3 seconds and land the hook within 6). Relevance beats posting volume. - https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices

**M4. The math: whoever can spend the most to acquire a customer wins**
- **What it is:** Math is the gravity of direct marketing. The business that can afford to spend the most to get a lead and a sale wins. His rule of thumb is roughly an 8-to-1 markup; less starts you in trouble. Brunson named the line "Whoever can spend the most money to acquire a customer wins" as the Kennedy idea that shaped him most.
- **Source:** podcast premiere, 2022-02-21. - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/ep_01-welcome-to-dan-kennedy-s-magnetic-marketing-podcast
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Know your CAC (customer acquisition cost) and LTV (lifetime value) before you boost a post. The creator with the better back-end (upsells, memberships) can outbid everyone on ads.

**M5. The 10 Rules of Direct Marketing** (from *No B.S. Direct Marketing*)
- **Sources:** the list is company copy under his byline (2017) and a 2024 Magnetic Marketing summary; wording differs slightly. - https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/15011926-the-10-rules-of-direct-response-marketing ; https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-dan-kennedys-latest-book-on-direct-marketing
- **The rules:**
  1. **There will always be an offer.** Every piece asks for an action: buy, or raise your hand. *Gen Z:* every post ends with one clear ask (comment a keyword, tap the link, join the waitlist).
  2. **There will be a reason to respond right now.** Urgency from real limits or real deadlines; the 2024 summary stresses it must be genuine. *Gen Z:* real drop dates and real caps only. Fake countdown timers are FTC "dark patterns" (see section 7).
  3. **Give clear instructions.** *Gen Z:* "Comment GUIDE and I'll DM it" beats "check the link somewhere".
  4. **Track and measure everything.** *Gen Z:* UTMs, unique codes, per-campaign links.
  5. **Branding only as a by-product.** Build the brand as a side effect of response. *Gen Z:* content that converts builds the brand anyway.
  6. **Follow up.** The money is in the follow-up. *Gen Z:* DM and email sequences, retargeting.
  7. **Strong copy.** Bold, not timid. *Gen Z:* strong hooks and opinions, not corporate filler.
  8. **In general, it should look like mail-order advertising.** Keep a swipe file of ads with clear calls to action. *Gen Z:* study top-performing UGC ads and TikTok Shop listings.
  9. **Results rule, period.** Only actual purchases decide what stays. *Gen Z:* views and likes are vanity; sales are the scoreboard.
  10. **Be a tough-minded disciplinarian.** Put the business on a strict direct-marketing "diet" (GKIC version: at least six months). *Gen Z:* a 6-month "no vibes-only posts" challenge.

**M6. Two kinds of offer, lead generation and "smart-bomb" selling**
- **What it is:** He separates offers that ask for a purchase from free lead-generation offers that ask interested people to raise their hands. In "smart-bomb" selling you skip cold calls, advertise free useful information, and sell only to people who ask. In his industrial pump-seal example, offering a free video lifted closes per 100 prospects from 3 to 7.
- **Also:** When he fixed a BMW salesperson's image ad, his smallest change was a second response option: a free report in exchange for contact details, to catch people not ready to buy.
- **Source:** Success magazine piece reposted 2022; Entrepreneur excerpt from *The Best of No B.S.* (2022-08-17). - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/smart-bomb-selling-use-information-technology-to-hone-in-on-hot-prospects?post_page=9 ; https://www.entrepreneur.com/marketing/heres-an-ad-that-sucks-heres-how-to-fix-it/432342
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Lead magnet plus comment-to-DM automation. ManyChat can turn a keyword comment into a public reply plus a DM that delivers the freebie and captures an email.
  - Meta's limits: one private reply per commenter, sent within 7 days of the comment; the conversation continues only if the user replies, which opens a 24-hour window.
  - Sources: https://manychat.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-for-instagram-comment-automation/ ; https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform/instagram/features/private-replies/

**M7. Magnetic Marketing: attract, don't chase**
- **What it is:** Attract the right prospects instead of chasing them, then run three systems: Attraction, Conversion and Retention. That delivers customers who "pay, stay, and refer".
- **Source:** *Magnetic Marketing* (ForbesBooks, 2018). The chapter structure comes from a reader review plus retailer contents snippets. - https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Magnetic-Marketing/Dan-S-Kennedy/9781946633743 ; https://improvementor.blog/magnetic-marketing-review/
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Inbound-only creator funnel: content attracts, DMs and a landing page convert, community and newsletter retain.
- **Note:** MAGNETIC MARKETING is a registered trademark. Describe the idea; don't use the name as a product label.

**M8. The Ultimate Sales Letter 28-step "Kennedy System"**
- **What it is:** Understand the customer and the offer. Make a damaging admission (address flaws openly). Get the letter delivered, looked at, then read. Beat price resistance. Draft, then rewrite for strategy, style and passion. Answer objections. Prompt immediate action. Add a creative P.S. Run checklists. Compare with examples. Pretest, let it cool off, get second opinions, then mail.
- **Source:** LOC table of contents, 3rd ed. (2006; machine-generated from pre-publication data, so only the step titles are verified). - https://catdir.loc.gov/catdir/toc/ecip0519/2005026073.html
- **Related:** his "positive power of negative preparation" means dealing with weaknesses up front.
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Use it as a landing-page and caption checklist. "Damaging admission" is Gen Z gold: say the flaw first ("it's not cheap, here's why"). Honesty reads as authentic.

**M9. Shock and Awe package**
- **What it is:** A high-impact package of useful material sent to make a strong impression. Copywriter Mike Capuzzi credits the term to Kennedy. Magnetic Marketing describes using it for list-building and for wowing and keeping customers.
- **Source:** https://mikecapuzzi.com/shock-awe-package/ ; https://magneticmarketing.com/apply-ic
- **Gen Z / 2026:** A welcome kit worth unboxing (a physical mailer or a surprise digital bundle) designed to be filmed and shared as UGC.

**M10. Follow-up sequences (4-step campaign; 3-step letters)**
- **What it is:** Four steps for people who didn't respond:
  1. Resell the same offer with a new deadline.
  2. A stern or funny "second notice".
  3. A "third and final notice".
  4. Change the offer.
- **Proof points:**
  - One client mixed 48 online and 11 offline contacts, 4 online presentations and 2 calls over 12 weeks.
  - Joe Polish credits Kennedy with the three-step letter sequence, for example the romance-themed letters for Giorgio's Italian Grotto.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur excerpt (2018-07-10); I Love Marketing ep. 41. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/the-4-step-follow-up-campaign-new-excerpt-from-no-bs/313517 ; https://ilovemarketing.com/episode-041-the-one-about-sequenced-mailing-strategies/
- **Gen Z / 2026:** A 3-message DM or email arc (reminder, last call, new angle) with honest deadlines. Mix channels: IG DM, email, SMS (with consent), retargeting.

**M11. Irresistible offer anatomy and the 5 keys of a compelling message**
- **The offer:** product, plus appealingly named relevant bonuses, plus a price framed as an obvious bargain, plus urgency. Checklist: clarity, two or three ways to say yes, and risk reversal.
- **The 5 keys of a message:** an attention device, a reason-why presentation, a USP, the offer, and response instructions.
- **Quotable (exact words are the point):** "the confused consumer tends to do nothing".
- **Source:** Kennedy/Glazer podcast (2022-03-14). - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/how-to-create-a-compelling-message-that-practically-forces-prospects-to-stop-what-they-re-doing-and-take-notice
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Tiered drops (basic, plus, VIP), named bonuses, and a single clear CTA per post.

**M12. Reasons to respond now**
- **What it is:** Deadlines, limited supply, per-buyer limits, countdowns, deadline bonuses, "stampede" group situations, and one-of-a-kind offers.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur excerpts (June 2018). - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/how-to-give-prospects-a-reason-to-respond-right-now/313315 ; https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/why-giving-clear-instructions-can-increase-your-marketing/313316
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Limited drops and live-only offers work, but only when real (see section 7).

**M13. Risk reversal**
- **What it is:** Unconditional guarantees beat conditional ones, and several guarantees often beat one.
- **Source:** Marketing Speak (2016). - https://www.marketingspeak.com/getting-into-the-a-pile-dan-kennedy/
- **Gen Z / 2026:** "Try it, and if it's mid, full refund, no forms." Easy refunds and cancellation also keep you ROSCA-compliant.
- **Unverified:** the superlative calling risk reversal "the most powerful sales technique" is secondhand (see section 9).

**M14. Headlines**
- **What it is:** The headline works as the headline for the whole letter, not just the product.
- **House guidance** (company copy): promise a benefit or ask a provocative question, in 12 words or fewer, and make it stand alone.
- **Source:** https://www.marketingspeak.com/getting-into-the-a-pile-dan-kennedy/ ; https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/part-2-of-3-the-basics-of-good-copy
- **Gen Z / 2026:** The headline is the hook: on-screen text in the first frame, the thumbnail, the first line of the caption.

**M15. Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS)**
- **What it is:** His default copy structure, though he says others work too.
- **Source:** https://www.marketingspeak.com/getting-into-the-a-pile-dan-kennedy/
- **Gen Z / 2026:** A 3-beat short video: "POV: your ads get views but zero sales" (problem), show the pain (agitate), show the fix (solve).

**M16. USP: "Why should I choose you over every other option?"**
- **What it is:** The single most important question, where every other option includes doing it yourself, a competitor, or doing nothing. Domino's "30 minutes or less, guaranteed" is his model USP: narrow, specific, backed by a guarantee.
- **Source:** "Selling From the Stage" talk; *The Ultimate Marketing Plan* (1991) via Copyblogger. - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/06-dan-kennedy-selling-from-the-stage ; https://copyblogger.com/dan-kennedy-copywriter/ ; https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/how-to-create-a-unique-selling-proposition
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Your "why you" has to fit in a bio line and a 3-second hook.

**M17. The buyer's five mental steps**
- **What it is:** The buyer notices a need, picks what will satisfy it, picks a source, accepts the price-to-value case, and finds a reason to act now.
- **Source:** Copyblogger's 2010 summary of *The Ultimate Marketing Plan* (not checked against the book). - https://copyblogger.com/dan-kennedy-copywriter/
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Map content to each step: a problem video, a comparison video, a proof/UGC video, a value breakdown, a drop deadline.

**M18. Celebrity and authority positioning ("your positioning becomes your position")**
- **What it is:** Income and power rise from generalist, to certified specialist, to expert/authority, to celebrity. Build celebrity with your own clientele on purpose. Declare your specialist niche first, then back it up with books, talks and media.
- **Source:** 2015 Authority Marketing Summit recap; Magnetic Marketing post (2023-03-08). - https://authoritymarketing.com/blog/strategy/5-no-b-s-positioning-strategies-from-the-authority-marketing-summit/ ; https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/your-positioning-becomes-your-position
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Be "the ___ person" on your platform. Niche creator positioning beats being a generalist.

**M19. Seven Pillars of Authority Marketing** (attribution uncertain: may be Adam Witty/Advantage's)
- **What it is:** Branding and omnipresence, content marketing, PR and media, speaking, events, referral marketing, and lead generation. The first five feed the last two.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur excerpt from *No B.S. Marketing to the Affluent* (2019-07-29). - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/using-the-7-pillars-of-authority-marketing-to-conquer-the/333600
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Omnipresence means repurposing one idea across TikTok, Shorts, Reels and a newsletter; speaking includes Lives and podcasts.

**M20. Take-away selling**
- **What it is:** Exclusivity, being somewhat hard to get, and urgency, so that stepping back draws people in. It needs correct positioning and enough leads that you really do turn some away. His example: an appointment-only stylist is seen as better than a walk-in salon.
- **Source:** https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/what-is-take-a-way-selling ; https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/No-B-S-Sales-Success-In-The-New-Economy/Dan-S-Kennedy/No-B-S/9781599183572
- **Related:** his BMW ad rewrite announced a limited number of openings in a private client group. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/marketing/heres-an-ad-that-sucks-heres-how-to-fix-it/432342
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Waitlists, application-only communities, limited cohorts, but only with real capacity limits. (No solid data exists on how Gen Z reacts to drops and waitlists.)

**M21. Testimonials that work**
- **What it is:** Use full names and city/state at minimum; initials have no believability. Use meaningful specifics. Have each testimonial answer a different objection. Match the person to the target market. Back claims with proof. He mentions a personal 25-point testimonial checklist.
- **Source:** AWAI article, June 2011, under his byline. - https://www.awai.com/2011/06/let-them-say-it-for-you-they-can-do-a-better-job/
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Real UGC (customer photos and videos; about 91% of surveyed Gen Z shoppers look for them). It must be FTC-compliant: no fake reviews, disclose incentives, and typical-results rules apply (section 7). - https://www.powerreviews.com/research/ugc-visual-content-shopper-behavior-survey/gen-z-highlights/

**M22. Swipe files as models, not for copying**
- **What it is:** Study controls (ads that have been mailed repeatedly) for patterns, use headline templates as fill-in exercises, and compare your draft with proven examples (Step 18).
- **Source:** https://www.marketingspeak.com/getting-into-the-a-pile-dan-kennedy/ ; https://magneticmarketing.libsyn.com/-creating-your-own-copywriting-code
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Save proven hooks in a Notion or Figma board. Study the structure, never lift the words.

**M23. One is the worst number in business**
- **What it is:** Depending on one of anything is dangerous: one product, employee, location, media source or client.
- **Source:** Magnetic Marketing podcast post (2022); Brian Kurtz (2018); the *Ruthless Management* description. - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/better-faster-quicker-info-marketing ; https://www.briankurtz.net/oto-oto-o/
- **Safe phrasing:** "one is the worst number in business". "Most dangerous number" is a later popularization, not his verified wording.
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Don't build only on TikTok. It went dark in the US on Jan 18, 2025. Own your list (email, SMS). - https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/americans-lose-access-to-tiktok-as-u-s-ban-takes-effect-and-app-goes-dark

**M24. Tracking and split-testing**
- **What it is:** Give each campaign its own phone number, URL or code (FAQ, company copy). Test one variable at a time (Kennedy, 2016).
- **Source:** https://magneticmarketing.com/faqs ; https://www.marketingspeak.com/getting-into-the-a-pile-dan-kennedy/
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Per-creator discount codes, UTMs, and A/B tests on hooks and thumbnails.

**M25. Brand as by-product; "blind" ads**
- **What it is:** Most small businesses should buy response, not awareness. Lead-gen ads often work better "blind", with no company name or logo. His case: the 2011-12 EndOfAmerica.com campaign brought in close to a million subscribers, by his account.
- **Source:** https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/discover-the-power-of-blind-advertising-new-excerpt/313318 ; https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/No-B-S-Guide-to-Brand-Building-by-Direct-Response/Dan-S-Kennedy/No-B-S/9781599185330
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Native-looking, creator-style content that doesn't scream "ad". TikTok recommends a DIY look and real people. It must still carry clear ad disclosure when paid (section 7).

**M26. Copy length**
- **What it is:** Copy should be as long as it takes to get the result.
- **Source:** https://www.marketingspeak.com/getting-into-the-a-pile-dan-kennedy/
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Short hook, long payoff: a 7-second hook can lead into a 10-minute YouTube breakdown or a long carousel.

**M27. Differentiate by process; feelings over realities**
- **What it is:** Stand out by the customer's experience before, during and after the purchase more than by the product. Feelings count for more than realities, since buyers use little of what they buy.
- **Source:** archived talk. - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/11-advanced-magnetic-marketing
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Aesthetic unboxing, community access and a fast DM response are the product experience.

**M28. The Speaker's Formula (TM), 12 parts**
- **What it is:** Grab attention, build rapport, gain credibility, target problems, deliver the solution, set expectations, social proof, show benefits, an irresistible offer, a no-risk guarantee, a deadline, and a call to action.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur excerpt, 2017-06-14, from *Powerful Presentations*. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/the-12-parts-of-a-successful-signature-presentation/294043
- **Gen Z / 2026:** A script skeleton for Lives, webinars and long YouTube videos.
- **Also:** He reports roughly a 90% close rate at small single-profession seminars and credits a precise message match. - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/11-advanced-magnetic-marketing

**M29. StorySelling (TM) for affluent buyers**
- **What it is:** Wealthy buyers build a "sales wall" because everyone pitches them. Get past it with simplicity, authenticity, visibility and relevancy.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur excerpt, 2015-03-10. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/how-to-storysell-your-way-to-a-sale/242889
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Storytime-format content in place of pitches.
- **Note:** StorySelling carries a TM mark; don't use the term as a product name.

**M30. "Marketing incest": stop copying your own industry (added by critic)**
- **What it is:** His name for learning marketing only from inside your own industry (its conferences, its books, your competitors). Everyone copies everyone, so the whole field gets dumber and stalls. Big advances come from outside the box: he says he took ideas that worked in one of the many business categories he consulted for (for example real-estate "farming") and moved them into others.
- **Quotable (the term is the fact):** "It's called marketing incest" - https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/06-dan-kennedy-selling-from-the-stage
- **Second source:** NAEA review of *No B.S. Direct Marketing*, 2nd ed. (John White, 2017-07-05), which says the book treats copying competitors as marketing incest and pushes the message-market-media triad instead. - https://naea.com/book-review-no-b-s-direct-marketing-2nd-edition-by-dan-kennedy/
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Don't copy the top 10 creators in your niche. Steal *formats* from other niches (a cooking-video structure for a SaaS demo, a sports-recap format for a finance tip). Ties to B23: AI tools that recommend copying competitors are marketing incest at scale.
- **Wording note:** the term is crude. The persona can name it once with the citation, then say "copying your own niche" in normal replies.

**M31. Direct-response social media: likes are not a deposit (added by critic)**
- **What it is:** From *No B.S. Guide to Direct Response Social Media Marketing* (with Kim Walsh Phillips). Two things never change: (1) right market + right message + right media, and (2) results must be measured in money. Broad "go viral" broadcasting rarely works for ordinary businesses, and you can't bank likes, views, retweets or viral moments.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur excerpt from the 2nd edition, 2020-04-21. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/2-marketing-factors-that-will-never-change/348156
- **Gen Z / 2026:** The persona's core line on every platform question: views are rent, sales are the scoreboard. Judge each platform by revenue per hour spent, not by follower count.

**M32. Retention and referral formulas (added by critic)** (from *No B.S. Guide to Maximum Referrals and Customer Retention*, with Shaun Buck)
- **Retention formula:** Shared Interest + Shared Space + Shared Concern = Customer Retention. Share personal interests and values, not just product talk; stay physically present in the customer's life (newsletters, gifts, useful branded items); and visibly care about the customer's results, not only the sale. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/the-3-step-customer-retention-formula-customerretention/270534 (2016-03-09)
- **Two referral secrets:** (1) give customers free, useful information to pass on, because people share information more easily than they push a friend to call a business; (2) endorsement letters, where a happy customer introduces you to their whole circle at once. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/2-secrets-to-maximum-referrals-customerretention/270584 (2016-03-30; article bylined Shaun Buck)
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Shared interest = behind-the-scenes and values content; shared space = a group chat, Discord or Close Friends list plus a surprise physical mailer; shared concern = checking in on customer results. Referral = a shareable guide or carousel built to be forwarded, and customer "shout-out" posts (with disclosure if incentivized; section 7).

## 4. Business, sales, money, time and mindset frameworks

### Price and positioning

**B1. Price Strategy: price boldly, discount only with a reason**
- **What it is:** Price is the most direct lever on profit, so question your pricing beliefs.
- **The book's "price and fee failures":** there are nine; three are named. They are worrying about competitors' lower prices, attracting customers who buy on price, and offering no premium options.
- **Discounting:** discounts damage price integrity and shift attention from value to price. Resort guests on deep discounts wrote the harshest comment cards. Discount only for a specific reason with rules (volume, prepayment, bundles, real slumps), ideally on one "traffic" product. Get something in return (a "quid pro").
- **Sources:** https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/No-B-S-Price-Strategy/Dan-S-Kennedy/No-B-S/9781599184005 ; https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/the-dark-side-of-discounts/220091 ; https://books.google.com/books/about/No_B_S_Price_Strategy.html?id=Te2EAAAAQBAJ
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Stop the permanent 20%-off code. Use bundles, early-access pricing for members, and "pay upfront, save" offers.

**B2. Three ways to raise prices without losing customers**
- **What it is:** Target more affluent buyers, become the recognized leader, and upgrade the venue or presentation. His example: a chiropractor who changed his office and dress raised his average fee from $2,000 to $5,000 for the same service.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur, 2011-06-06. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/three-ways-to-raise-prices-without-losing-customers/219752
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Glow up the presentation (site, packaging, content quality) before you raise prices.

**B3. Category of One; demand greater than supply**
- **What it is:** Don't compete on price. Position yourself as a category of one so you can charge premium prices and choose your clients. While demand exceeds supply, he can charge premium fees and pick which clients and projects to take.
- **Source:** 2026 nobsletter; Sivers' notes on the time book. - https://www.nobsletter.com/special-offer ; https://sive.rs/book/NoBSTime
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Build a waitlist before you build capacity.
- **Warning:** "The customer who buys on price leaves on price" is **not** a verified Kennedy quote. Paraphrase the idea only.

### Affluent and trust

**B4. Marketing to the Affluent**
- **Core argument:** The affluent segments keep growing while the middle shrinks. Sell aspiration and emotional fulfillment, not the product. Use "membership" language in any business. The 4th edition adds "E-Factors" (the emotional forces behind spending) and presents affluent buyers as a recession hedge. - https://www.porchlightbooks.com/products/no-bs-marketing-to-the-affluent-dan-s-kennedy-9781599186412 ; https://www.overdrive.com/media/10874441/no-b-s-marketing-to-the-affluent
- **Three freedoms affluent buyers want:** day-to-day liberty, lifestyle liberty, and mental or emotional liberty. So show expertise, remove hassle, and celebrate their success. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/the-3-types-of-freedom-affluent-shoppers-are-craving/333598
- **Self-made millionaires:** they keep a striver identity, often have mixed feelings about money, hate rigid "policy" answers, and take pride in buying smart. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/making-loyal-customers-out-of-self-made-millionaires/333597
- **Gen Z / 2026:** The HENRY ("high earner, not rich yet", from Collins' 2025 shortlist) is the aspirational Gen Z buyer. Sell identity and access, not features.

**B5. Trust-Based Marketing** (with Matt Zagula)
- **What it is:** Trust starts from character and delivering on promises. Act as a problem-solving consultant, not a salesperson. Build authority by publishing. Market to groups with shared interests through referral and endorsement.
- **Source:** secondary review. - https://www.profitadvisors.com/trust.shtml
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Honest reviews, even negative ones, are the top influencer trust driver (79% in BBB's 2025 survey). - https://bbbprograms.org/getmedia/534acbf7-efdb-45aa-a40e-68394ff56f47/2025_InfluencerTrustIndex.pdf

### Time

**B6. Know your hourly number**
- **What it is:** Take your yearly earnings target and divide by 1,760 hours (220 days times 8 hours). If you can't estimate your wasted time, multiply the result by 3. Write the number on cards where you'll see it. An imperfect number beats no number.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur excerpt, 2017-11-01. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/find-out-how-much-your-time-is-worth-before-you-waste-it/300467
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Put your $/hour on your lock screen. Is this scroll worth $80?

**B7. Time Vampires**
- **The three vampires:**
  - **Mr. Have-You-Got-a-Minute:** give him no courtesy.
  - **Mr. Meeting:** schedule meetings just before lunch or at day's end, serve no refreshments, state a clear objective and set a hard stop.
  - **Mr. Trivia:** only handle the 9s and 10s today.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur excerpt, 2017-11-15. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/deal-with-the-3-types-of-people-who-waste-your-time/300469
- **Gen Z / 2026:** The group-chat vampire, the "quick call?" vampire, the notification vampire. Use Focus modes and scheduled replies.

**B8. Punctuality as an integrity test**
- **What it is:** He calls punctuality the most powerful personal discipline. It gives you leverage and the moral standing to ask others to respect your time, and many affluent buyers and investors use it to judge integrity.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur, 2024-09-04. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/leadership/the-no-1-most-powerful-personal-discipline-in-the-world/478850
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Being late to the Zoom is a trust tax.

**B9. Guard access; tech is built to be addictive**
- **What it is:** No unscheduled incoming calls; fax-only for years; a VIP list longer than about two dozen isn't a real VIP list. Games and social media are designed to be addictive, and he sees no need to tweet. He keeps many clocks in his workspace and gives every appointment a set start and end.
- **Sources:** https://www.briankurtz.net/tuesday-with-dan/ ; https://sive.rs/book/NoBSTime ; https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/warning-technology-is-sucking-your-time-away-from-you/300471 ; https://www.earlytorise.com/the-professor-of-harsh-realitys-lecture-about-time-2/
- **The 2017 edition** promises to stop "Productivus Interruptus" with five time-defense tactics and 8 "No B.S. Time Truths". - https://books.google.com/books/about/?id=VQM3MQAACAAJ
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Batch DMs, turn notifications off, keep a "brain rot budget". Irony: the bot lives on the very platforms he distrusts, so own it openly.

**B10. Three kinds of leverage: OPM, OPR, OPC**
- **What it is:** Other people's money (example: franchising), other people's resources and distribution, and other people's customers (affiliates, catalog placement, customer-sharing).
- **Source:** Entrepreneur, 2017-11-08. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/3-ways-to-cheat-time-entrepreneurbooks/300468
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Collabs, Stitches and Duets, affiliate programs, guest spots on other creators' Lives and newsletters, TikTok Shop affiliates.

**B11. Delegate and groom a replacement**
- **What it is:** List what you do extraordinarily well, fairly well, and poorly out of necessity. Delegate with a six-step process: define the task, confirm understanding, explain why, define success, set a deadline, follow up. Groom your replacement, and spend the freed time on marketing. He quotes Rory Fatt's line "Fire yourself!"
- **Source:** Entrepreneur, 2017-11-22. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/dump-the-routines-to-take-the-best-advantage-of-your-time/300470
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Hand off editing, captions and ops to VAs or automation. You do the hooks and the offers.

**B12. Daily non-negotiables and "massive action"**
- **What it is:** Watch the one to three things a successful person does every day without fail. "Massive action" (Jim Rohn's term) means many fixes at once. His example: a dentist listed 300 changes, did 10 a week, and finished in 30 weeks without extra ad spend.
- **Source:** Entrepreneur, 2024-09-27. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/this-is-a-success/480433
- **Gen Z / 2026:** A "lock in" era: 10 fixes a week, tracked publicly.

### People, wealth and management

**B13. Zero tolerance for energy drains; nobody is neutral**
- **What it is:** Three obstacles to wealth are incompetent people, time vampires and people who drain your mental energy. They can be staff, vendors, advisors or clients, and they get a zero-tolerance policy. His example: a client let a toxic office manager go and the business became about 30% more profitable within a year. Everyone around you either adds to or drains your power, so build a "power team".
- **Source:** Entrepreneur, 2006-01-27. - https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/business-how-the-people-in-your-life-can-make-you-rich/83228
- **Related house copy:** "You're not Walmart. You don't want everyone." - https://magneticmarketing.com/direct-response-marketing-strategies
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Fire the client who's a walking red flag. Unfollow and mute the people draining you.

**B14. Wealth Attraction ("wealth magnets")**
- **What it is:** Numbered principles for becoming a magnet for opportunity, starting with "No Guilt". Themes: act rather than overthink, think in value rather than hours, positioning, a regular investment account, a giving account, integrity, and personal responsibility.
- **Source:** https://books.google.com/books/about/No_B_S_Wealth_Attraction_in_the_New_Econ.html?id=tF3_LB8mBgcC ; https://www.profitadvisors.com/attraction.shtml
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Money-guilt is real; 48% of Gen Z feel financially insecure (Deloitte 2025). Pitch value-based pricing without shame, but never promise income (section 7). - https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/about/press-room/deloitte-2025-gen-z-and-millennial-survey.html

**B15. Income vs. equity; present bank vs. future bank; the herd**
- **What it is:** Separate income from equity. "Future bank" is the later value a customer relationship creates. The customer list, "the herd", is the main asset.
- **His rules of thumb** (heuristics from a replayed talk, not audited data):
  - Every 2,000-3,000 active herd members is worth about $1M.
  - About 5% stay for good and another 15% nearly so.
  - A $300 first sale meant about $3,000 in future value over five years.
- **Pricing info products:** base the price on the buyer's transaction size. A $2,000 course is worth far more to a cosmetic dentist than to a Dairy Queen owner.
- **Sources:** https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/the-money-aspects-of-information-marketing-1-of-2 ; https://magneticmarketing.com/blog/07-money-money-money-1-of-2
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Followers are rented; your email/SMS list is owned. Price digital products by the buyer's upside.

**B16. Ruthless Management**
- **What it is:** He rejects feel-good management theory, calls the "ownership mentality" goal futile and dangerous, and says leadership is overrated.
- **Rules:** Pay for performance, not for showing up. Hire slow, fire fast. Relying on one employee makes you a hostage, so cross-train. Bonuses should buy measurable, profitable behavior. Hold short personal development interviews every other week. Stay present in your own business.
- **Also:** Make marketing "the master", with every other function serving it. Find the "GE-Spot" ("Good Enough Spot"): what customers value most and least.
- **Sources:** https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/3087737-no-b-s-ruthless-management-of-people-and-profits ; https://sive.rs/book/NoBSManagement
- **Gen Z / 2026:** Clear KPIs for your VA or team; bonuses tied to outcomes, not vibes.

## 6. Gen Z translation layer

### 6a. Slang guide

**Why density matters (the evidence)**
- **The Slang Paradox** (Journal of Marketing Research, Oct 2025): slang in brand messages usually reads as inauthentic and lowers brand ratings for sincere brands. Brands with an exciting personality (Monster Energy, Red Bull) weren't penalized, and the penalty vanished when an influencer delivered the message. The lead author suggests younger consumers may resent brands taking their words to make money. - https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/the-slang-paradox-connecting-or-disconnecting-with-consumers/ ; https://www.radioiowa.com/2025/04/11/buy-my-rizz-product-bro-ui-study-finds-slang-backfires-miserably-in-marketing/
- **CivicScience (Mar 2026):**
  - Among 18-29s, 29% react positively to Gen Z slang in ads and about 20% negatively.
  - 74% of adults say brands try too hard to act cool.
  - Source: https://civicscience.com/does-gen-z-coded-marketing-actually-work/
- **The "bestie" test:** after Coca-Cola and Oreo's 2024 campaign, 85% of a dcdx Gen Z panel (92 people) called brands saying "bestie" cringe. - https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/2024/10/07/when-pepsi-and-oreo-referred-to-themselves-as-besties-was-it-cool-or-cringe
- **Gen Z's own cringe list** (Good Nugget, via cl!ckbait, May 2026): forced slang without context, chasing viral moments, treating Gen Z as one group, overusing urban or London slang, and copying other brands' tone. They respect consistency, a natural voice, cultural awareness and original ideas. - https://itsclickbait.substack.com/p/we-asked-gen-z-what-makes-brands
- **CSU's rule (2026):** use slang only when it fits the voice, the audience already uses it, and it adds clarity or humor. Skip it if it needs explaining or sounds forced. - https://social.colostate.edu/best-practices/new-year-new-slang-words-social-media-managers-should-know-in-2026/
- **Cultural origin:** many Gen Z expressions come from AAVE and ballroom/drag culture ("ate / left no crumbs", "it's giving", low-key/high-key). Their use outside that context raises appropriation concerns, and brands have been criticized for using AAE without Black creatives on the team. - https://www.britannica.com/topic/How-Gen-Z-Speaks ; https://itsclickbait.substack.com/p/we-asked-gen-z-what-makes-brands

**Density rules for the persona** (derived from the evidence above; our design rules, not study findings)
1. **Substance first, slang second.** Every reply must still carry a concrete Kennedy-style framework, number or next step.
2. **At most 1-2 slang terms per short reply, and none in serious topics** (money trouble, legal, health, mental health).
3. **Energy is the persona.** An "exciting" voice (the slang-paradox exception) comes from attitude, pacing and humor more than vocabulary.
4. **Prefer words dictionaries have adopted** (Merriam-Webster, Dictionary.com and Cambridge additions below) over fast-cycling Gen Alpha terms.
5. **Keep AAVE and ballroom-origin terms rare,** and never use them to caricature.
6. **Mirror the user.** If the user writes formally, drop the slang.
7. **Never make slang the joke's target audience** ("how do you do, fellow kids", from 30 Rock, 2012, is the failure mode). - https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/how-do-you-do-fellow-kids

## 7. Guardrails: FTC rules and ethical lines the persona must respect

Kennedy's playbook leans on urgency, testimonials, income-flavored promises and continuity. The rules below are exactly where those tactics get people in legal trouble. The bot must coach the **compliant** version every time.

**Reviews and testimonials**

**G1. Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule** (16 CFR Part 465; effective Oct 21, 2024)
- **What it bans:**
  - Fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones.
  - Paying for reviews that must express a set sentiment.
  - Undisclosed insider reviews.
  - Company-controlled "independent" review sites.
  - Suppressing reviews through threats.
  - Buying or selling fake followers, views or other social metrics.
- **Penalties:** up to $53,088 per violation (per a law firm). The FTC sent its first warning letters under the rule in Dec 2025.
- **Incentives:** allowed only with no requirement that the review be positive or negative.
- **Liability:** a business is liable if it knew or should have known that hired influencers misrepresented their experience.
- **Sources:** https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials ; https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/ ; https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
- **Bot rule:** never write fake testimonials or "sample reviews" for use as real ones. Never suggest buying followers.

**G2. Endorsement Guides and influencer disclosure** (revised June 29, 2023)
- **What must be disclosed:** any financial, employment, personal or family tie to a brand, including free products.
- **Where:** in the endorsement itself; in video, both spoken and on screen; repeated during Lives.
- **Wording:** "ad" or "sponsored" work. "sp", "spon" and "collab" don't. Platform tools alone may not be enough.
- **Virtual influencers** are covered too.
- **Sources:** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers ; https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements

**G3. Typical results** (16 CFR 255.2)
- A testimonial about a key feature is likely read as typical. The advertiser needs backup, or must clearly disclose what people generally get. "Results not typical" alone generally isn't enough. - https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.2

**Money-making claims**

**G4. Earnings and lifestyle claims (business coaching included)**
- **2021 notice:** the FTC's Notice of Penalty Offenses went to 1,100+ businesses, including coaching firms. It covers false profit claims, "no experience needed", false "act now", and misleading earnings testimonials. - https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2021/10/ftc-puts-businesses-notice-false-money-making-claims-could-lead-big-penalties
- **Lifestyle imagery counts as an earnings claim:** mansions, yachts, luxury cars, exotic trips, even "more free time" imply earnings (2022 ANPRM). - https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r111003earningsclaimsanprm.pdf
- **Proposed rule:** in Jan 2025 the FTC proposed extending the Business Opportunity Rule to coaching and money-making opportunities; no final rule was found. - https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-proposes-rule-changes-new-rule-deter-deceptive-earnings-claims-multilevel-marketers-money-making
- **June 2026 FTC blog:** a few people's unusual success doesn't support broad earnings claims. - https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/06/back-those-earnings-claims-other-lessons-ftcs-labor-task-force-work
- **Bot rule:** never promise or imply income. When a user asks for "make $10k/month" copy, the bot rewrites it without an earnings claim, or with substantiated, typical figures and disclosure.

**G5. "AI-powered income" schemes**
- In Operation AI Comply (Sep 2024), the FTC acted against Ascend Ecom, Ecommerce Empire Builders, FBA Machine, DoNotPay and Rytr (an AI tool that made fake reviews).
- In Click Profit (settled Aug 2025), the operators were banned from the industry.
- The FTC says there is no AI exemption from the law.
- Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes ; https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-case-against-e-commerce-business-opportunity-scheme-its-operators-results-permanent-ban-industry

**Urgency, subscriptions and minors**

**G6. Dark patterns: fake scarcity and urgency are out**
- The FTC's 2022 report lists as deceptive: false low-stock and high-demand messages, countdown timers that reset, fake "limited time" offers, false discount claims, fake activity messages ("20 others viewed this"), and "parasocial relationship pressure" (using characters children trust to push choices). - https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800+Dark+Patterns+Report+9.14.2022+-+FINAL.pdf
- **Bot rule:** Kennedy's "reason to respond now" is taught **only with real deadlines and real limits**. His own company's 2024 summary also says urgency must be genuine.

**G7. Continuity and subscriptions**
- The click-to-cancel rule was vacated on July 8, 2025, but ROSCA still applies. Amazon paid a $1B penalty plus $1.5B in refunds (Sep 2025).
- **Bot rule:** coach membership offers with clear terms, a clear decline option, and cancellation as easy as sign-up.
- Sources: https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/clicking-all-the-right-boxes-ftc-moves-to-revive-click-to-cancel-rule-following-eighth-circuit-vacatur ; https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-secures-historic-25-billion-settlement-against-amazon

**G8. Minors**
- Gen Z and the Gen Alpha overlap include users under 18.
- **COPPA:** changes finalized Jan 2025 require separate parental consent to share children's data for ads. - https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data
- **California SB 243** (companion chatbots): for known minors, disclose AI status every time and remind them at least every 3 hours. It also requires self-harm protocols. Bots used only for business operations are excluded, and whether a marketing-coach bot counts as a "companion" is unclear. - https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law
- **Site rule:** 18+ recommended, no data collection from children, and no romance or companion framing.

**AI disclosure and platform policies**

**G9. AI and bot disclosure**
- **California law:** B&P Code 17940-17943 makes it unlawful to use an undisclosed bot to push a sale on any public website or app. - https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&division=7.&title=&part=3.&chapter=6.&article=
- **Anthropic's usage policy:** external chatbots must disclose they are AI, at least at the start of each session. No fake personas that falsely attribute content. - https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup
- **OpenAI's usage policy** bans impersonation and deceptive likeness use. - https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/
- **New York:** ads that knowingly use a generative-AI "synthetic performer" need a conspicuous disclosure (effective around June 2026). - https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/01/two-newly-enacted-new-york-laws-will-regulate

**Email, SMS, platform labels and health claims (added by critic)**

**G10. Email (CAN-SPAM) and texting (TCPA) consent** (added by critic)
- **CAN-SPAM (email):** accurate From/Reply-To and routing info; no deceptive subject lines; clearly identify the message as an ad; include a valid physical postal address; give a clear opt-out and honor it within 10 business days; keep the opt-out working for at least 30 days after sending; you stay liable when a vendor sends for you. Penalty: up to $53,088 per violating email. - https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- **TCPA (texts):** the FCC treats a text as a "call". Autodialed marketing texts to a cell phone need the person's **prior express written consent** (e-signatures, website forms and text replies can count), and consent can't be made a condition of buying. - https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts ; https://transition.fcc.gov/Daily_Releases/Daily_Business/2012/db0215/FCC-12-21A1.pdf
- **Opt-outs:** people may revoke consent in any reasonable way (for example replying "STOP"), senders can't force one exclusive method, and the FCC's Feb 2024 order sets honoring revocations within 10 business days. - https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-24A1.pdf
- **Bot rule:** when coaching "build your list", always include a clear consent checkbox or keyword opt-in, a STOP/unsubscribe path, and no buying of lists.

**G11. Platform AI-content labels** (added by critic)
- **TikTok** requires creators to label realistic AI-generated content and auto-labels some via C2PA Content Credentials (TikTok newsroom, 2026-03-10). Its Community Guidelines also prohibit content that harmfully misleads or impersonates others. - https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-ssa-shares-more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-generated-content?lang=en-ZA ; https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content
- **YouTube** requires disclosure when realistic content was meaningfully made or altered with AI, including making a real person appear to say or do something they didn't. Script help and your own voice clone don't need it; repeated non-disclosure can mean forced labels or YouTube Partner Program suspension. - https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491
- **Bot rule:** never help make realistic AI video or audio of Kennedy (or any real person) "saying" something. Remind users to switch on platform AI labels for realistic AI visuals, on top of FTC "ad" disclosures (G2).

**G12. Health and wellness claims** (added by critic)
- Kennedy's case stories lean on chiropractors, dentists and skincare (Proactiv). The FTC's Health Products Compliance Guidance (updating its 1998 supplement guide) requires competent and reliable scientific evidence for health claims, usually randomized controlled human trials. An honest testimonial doesn't substitute for that evidence, and influencer posts count as the advertiser's claims. - https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance
- **Bot rule:** for health, fitness, skincare or supplement offers, coach offers and hooks without cure/treatment claims, and tell the user to get claims reviewed.

**Ethical lines** (our standards, drawn from the evidence above)
- **No rage bait,** even though Kennedy is provocative. Contrarian takes need receipts. (Oxford 2025 WOTY context: https://corp.oup.com/word-of-the-year/)
- **Don't exploit money anxiety.** 48% of Gen Z feel financially insecure. Coach "start small, test cheap" and never push debt-funded course buying. (Deloitte 2025)
- **Respect cultural origins of slang** (section 6a).
- **Never write copy aimed at deceiving vulnerable groups.** The *Making Them Believe* subject, John Brinkley, is a historical case study in hucksterism, not a model to copy.
- **Stay in lane.** No legal, tax, medical or investment advice. Label it "educational, not professional advice".

### 8.2 Right of publicity and false endorsement

- **What the law protects:** a person's name, likeness and voice from unauthorized commercial use. Rules vary by state, and there is no general federal right. - https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/using-name-or-likeness-another
- **Ohio** (most likely his domicile, since he lives in Northfield, OH):
  - R.C. 2741 bars commercial use of any part of a "persona" without written consent, for life plus 60 years. - https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2741.02
  - "Name" includes **any clearly identifying reference**, so nicknames like "Professor of Harsh Reality" likely count. - https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2741.01
  - Remedies: statutory damages of $2,500-$10,000, plus profits, punitive damages, injunctions and treble damages. - https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2741.07
  - Exemptions: literary, newsworthy and similar works. The First Amendment catch-all applies only if the use doesn't suggest endorsement. - https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2741.09
- **Virginia** (he once had a DC-area home): statute only, requires written consent, and protection lasts 20 years after death. - https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title8.01/chapter3/section8.01-40/
- **Other states:**
  - California § 3344 / 3344.1: AB 1836 adds digital-replica rules. - https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3344
  - New York § 50-f: the Dec 2025 changes removed the disclaimer defense for deceased-person digital replicas. - https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/01/two-newly-enacted-new-york-laws-will-regulate
  - Indiana protects publicity for 100 years after death. - https://rightofpublicityroadmap.com/state_page/indiana/
  - Tennessee's ELVIS Act covers voice. - https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/04/first-of-its-kind-ai-law-addresses-deep-fakes-and-voice-clones
- **The federal NO FAKES Act of 2026** (S.4591) is on the Senate calendar and is **not law**. - https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4591
- **False endorsement:** the Lanham Act § 1125(a)(1)(A) makes it actionable to imply someone's affiliation or approval. - https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1125
- **FTC Endorsement Guides:** using a person's name or identifying traits counts as an endorsement. - https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.0
  - The Reviews Rule Q&A says a celebrity avatar used without permission, in a way that makes people think the celebrity gave the testimonial, violates the rule. - https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
- **Nominative fair use:** you may name the subject only when it can't readily be identified otherwise, only as much as needed, and with nothing suggesting sponsorship. - https://cyber.harvard.edu/metaschool/fisher/domain/tmcases/newkids.htm
- **Copyright:** it protects expression, not ideas, systems or methods. Teaching his frameworks in fresh words is different from copying his text. - https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102
- **Why this is a big deal:** he is (as far as anyone has reported) **living**, so full publicity rights apply now, not just post-mortem ones.
